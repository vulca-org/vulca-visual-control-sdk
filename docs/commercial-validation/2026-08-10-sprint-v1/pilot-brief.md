# VULCA Creative Operations Pilot

Status: commercial hypothesis for design-partner validation

Default duration: five working days

Default mode: concierge, no integration required

## One-Sentence Promise

VULCA helps a multi-brand creative team carry brief constraints, review
decisions, adoption context, and permission boundaries from one visual batch
into the next.

## Ideal Design Partner

The strongest initial partner is a creative, paid-social, UGC, localisation,
or cross-border agency that:

- manages several visual candidates per brief;
- has at least two review or handoff roles;
- already mixes AI, creator, design, and editing tools;
- experiences repeated explanation, revision, or approval work;
- can provide one active, public, synthetic, or non-sensitive batch;
- is willing to compare a first batch with a second batch.

The likely buyer or internal champion is a Head/Director of Creative
Operations, Head of Production, Creative Technology Lead, AI Innovation Lead,
or founder/CEO in a smaller agency.

## Pilot Inputs

- one bounded creative brief;
- 10–20 candidate images or short-form visual assets;
- existing brand, product, channel, and exclusion requirements;
- named review and approval roles;
- a second small batch where accepted constraints can be reused;
- explicit handling rules for confidential, personal, licensed, or restricted
  material.

## Five-Day Flow

### Day 0 — Scope And Baseline

- Confirm the task, people, assets, sources, and permitted uses.
- Record the current review process, typical rounds, elapsed time, and repeated
  explanations.
- Freeze the pilot success metrics before VULCA work begins.

### Day 1 — Brief And Constraint Record

- Convert the brief into reviewable requirements and exclusions.
- Separate observed facts, supplied requirements, inferences, and unresolved
  questions.
- Record owner and permission routes instead of treating the system as an
  automated approver.

### Day 2 — Review-Ready Batch

- Produce one compact row per candidate: requirement, visible issue, evidence
  or source, proposed action, owner, and status.
- Preserve alternatives where the evidence does not justify one definitive
  answer.

### Day 3 — Human Decision

- The partner accepts, rejects, restricts, or revises candidate decisions.
- Record who decided, why, what was adopted, and what cannot be reused.
- AI-assisted analysis remains draft until a named human confirms it.

### Day 4 — Second-Batch Reuse Test

- Reuse only the accepted constraints and decisions on a second small batch.
- Measure what the team no longer needs to explain and where human review is
  still required.

### Day 5 — Readout

- Compare baseline and second-batch metrics.
- Identify reusable value, false assumptions, unresolved risks, and the next
  smallest commercial step.

## Deliverables

- brief and constraint record;
- candidate review table;
- decision and adoption log;
- owner/permission route list;
- reusable constraint pack for batch two;
- before/after workflow readout;
- explicit list of unsupported claims and unresolved questions.

## Metrics

At least one time metric and one quality-of-handoff metric should be frozen
before the pilot:

- time from batch receipt to first review;
- total review rounds;
- number of requirements re-explained;
- number of decisions with an attributable reason and owner;
- number of accepted constraints reused in batch two;
- partner-reported confidence in handoff clarity;
- willingness to continue, pay, or introduce another owner.

These are pilot measurements, not guaranteed outcomes.

## Explicit Non-Claims

The pilot does not claim:

- automated approval, legal clearance, rights clearance, or claim validation;
- autonomous generation of all campaign assets;
- customer adoption before a customer actually agrees;
- model training, parameter updating, or proven continual learning;
- improved campaign performance, conversion, or revenue;
- that public or fictional examples represent a named company's materials.

## Buyer-Facing Short Version

> VULCA is looking for two design partners running multi-asset creative
> workflows. In a five-day, no-integration pilot, we take one small visual
> batch, organise its brief constraints and review decisions into a compact
> handoff record, and then test whether the accepted decisions reduce repeated
> explanation on a second batch. The workflow keeps human approval and
> permission boundaries explicit. It is not automated approval or a claim that
> a model learns by itself.

## Discovery Questions

1. How many candidates are normally produced for one brief?
2. Who reviews first, and who can approve final use?
3. Where are requirements, revision reasons, and adoption decisions recorded?
4. What has to be explained again when the model, creator, editor, market, or
   channel changes?
5. Which cost is largest: elapsed time, revision effort, client confidence,
   brand inconsistency, or rights uncertainty?
6. What measurable result would make a second pilot or paid continuation
   worthwhile?
