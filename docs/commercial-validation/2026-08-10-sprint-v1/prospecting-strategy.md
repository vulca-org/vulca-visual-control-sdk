## 1. What The 90 Accounts Are For

The 90-account list is a research universe, not a 90-message campaign. Its job
is to give VULCA enough coverage to test three separate questions:

1. Who can adopt a five-day creative-operations pilot with little procurement
   or integration friction?
2. Where is the repeated-brief, review, localisation, or handoff problem most
   operationally expensive?
3. Which adjacent platforms or production systems already cover part of the
   proposed value, changing whether VULCA should sell, partner, integrate, or
   differentiate?

## 2. Portfolio Design: 30 + 30 + 30

### Portfolio A — 30 Design-Partner Targets

These are smaller or mid-sized creative, social, creator, UGC, paid-media, and
performance-creative operators. They should have:

- high visual-batch frequency;
- at least two handoff or review roles;
- a founder, operations, production, creative, or innovation owner;
- a current public business route;
- enough autonomy to try one five-day, no-integration workflow test.

Primary objective: secure a qualified discovery conversation and one bounded
design-partner pilot.

### Portfolio B — 30 Adjacent Enterprise Targets

These are larger production, localisation, adaptation, ecommerce-content, CGI,
and network-agency operations. They should have a stronger underlying problem
but usually carry greater routing, procurement, security, or integration
friction.

Primary objective: test enterprise pain, vocabulary, buyer ownership, and the
minimum procurement-safe wedge. A reply does not establish adoption.

### Portfolio C — 30 Strategic-Learning Accounts

These are creative-operations, DAM, brand-governance, review-and-approval,
marketing-resource-management, or creative-automation companies.

Primary objective: determine whether the correct route is customer,
integration partner, channel partner, competitor, or no-go. They must not be
treated as ordinary buyers, and confidential implementation details should not
be disclosed.

## 3. Candidate Extraction Funnel

Every account passes the same eight-stage funnel.

### Stage 1 — Seed From A Workflow, Not A Category Label

Candidate generation starts from visible workflows such as:

- many assets per campaign or brief;
- creator, AI, design, video, and editing tools used together;
- market, language, format, or channel adaptation;
- explicit briefing, review, approval, or versioning stages;
- a second production or optimisation round;
- distributed internal, client, creator, and production roles.

Being an “AI company”, “creative agency”, or famous brand is not sufficient.

### Stage 2 — Normalize The Current Entity

Resolve acquisitions, parent companies, rebrands, retired brands, and regional
entities before ranking. The current legal or operating entity is recorded; a
retired name is not approached as if it were independent.

### Stage 3 — Verify The Operational Problem Signal

Look for current first-party evidence that the company actually handles a
multi-asset or multi-reviewer workflow. Public product and service positioning
is a fit signal, not proof that the company experiences the proposed pain.

### Stage 4 — Find The Accountable Person

Preferred personas, in order:

1. Head/VP/Director of Creative Operations or Production;
2. COO, operations leader, or transformation leader;
3. creative-technology, product-innovation, or applied-AI leader;
4. chief creative officer or executive creative director;
5. founder/CEO for a smaller independent operator.

PR, press, careers, and generic marketing contacts are not treated as buyer
owners merely because their email is visible.

### Stage 5 — Verify A Non-Guessed Route

Accepted routes are:

- an explicitly published business, partnerships, new-business, or team email;
- an official business, demo, partnership, or contact form;
- a current parent-company route where the brand is no longer independent.

Personal email patterns are never inferred. Support-only, press-only, or
careers-only routes are downgraded or excluded.

### Stage 6 — Record A Current Trigger

Each account needs one specific opening grounded in a current official source,
for example a new operations leader, AI workflow, production model, acquisition,
localisation system, or review-and-approval product. Generic praise is not a
personalisation trigger.

### Stage 7 — Score Out Of Ten

Each dimension is worth two points:

- 0–2: clear multi-asset, multi-market, or multi-reviewer workflow;
- 0–2: current AI, creator, localisation, adaptation, or scale signal;
- 0–2: named accountable person with a current source-backed role;
- 0–2: public, relevant, non-guessed route;
- 0–2: specific current trigger supporting a tailored opening.

Score meaning:

- `9–10`: cohort candidate after legal and sender checks;
- `8`: strong reserve requiring one route or persona confirmation;
- `6–7`: adjacent or strategic-learning account, not immediate send;
- `<6`: exclude from the operational pool.

### Stage 8 — Allocate By Commercial Relationship

Every account is labelled `buyer`, `partner`, `competitor`, or
`strategic_learning`. A company with exact product overlap can score highly on
workflow fit while remaining a poor cold-pilot target.

## 4. Source Hierarchy

Use sources in this order:

1. current official leadership, team, service, product, and contact pages;
2. current official company announcements and event-speaker pages;
3. current official authored articles confirming a person's role;
4. public professional profiles only when the company site is incomplete;
5. directories and search snippets for discovery only, never as the sole basis
   for a high-confidence row.

Every role and route is a dated snapshot and must be rechecked immediately
before use.

## 5. Exclusion And Suppression Rules

Exclude or quarantine:

- all 30 companies contacted in June 2026 for the duration of this sprint;
- any prior bounced route;
- duplicate subsidiaries where the same parent route and owner would receive
  the same message;
- inactive, retired, or absorbed brands without a current successor route;
- sole traders or protected recipient types where the required consent or
  direct-marketing basis is absent;
- support, press, careers, or investor routes with no legitimate business-
  enquiry purpose;
- opted-out, disputed, or negatively responding recipients;
- accounts where the message would require unsupported customer, performance,
  approval, compliance, or continual-learning claims.

## 6. Sending Sequence: Six Cohorts, Not One Blast

The 90-account universe is activated only in cohorts of ten.

1. `A1`: ten highest-fit, highest-route-confidence design-partner targets;
2. inspect delivery, human replies, wrong-owner redirects, and objections;
3. `A2`: ten targets chosen to test the strongest revised hypothesis;
4. `A3`: final ten Portfolio A accounts only if the offer still survives;
5. activate Portfolio B only when Portfolio A reveals a real enterprise pain or
   a specific larger-account route;
6. approach Portfolio C selectively for partnership or category learning, not
   with the same buyer email.

One follow-up maximum after 4–5 working days. No attachment on first touch.

## 7. Decision Rules

- After each 10-account cohort: if there are no human replies, stop and inspect
  sender reputation, route quality, role ownership, trigger specificity, and
  offer clarity before sending again.
- After 30 Portfolio A accounts: if there is no qualified discovery
  conversation, do not continue through the remaining 60 by inertia; reframe
  or kill the wedge.
- After five discovery conversations: if fewer than three describe a shared
  repeated-brief, review, or handoff problem, change the ICP or offer.
- After one or two pilots: if the second batch does not reduce repeated
  explanation, review rounds, or handoff effort, reframe or kill the workflow-
  memory promise.

## 8. What The List Does Not Prove

A 90-account list proves only that a researched prospect universe exists. It
does not prove:

- that a company experiences the hypothesised problem;
- that the named person is willing or able to buy;
- that an official route will reach the named person;
- that an email was delivered or read;
- customer adoption, revenue, pilot success, or market demand;
- model improvement, parameter updating, or continual learning.
